Mammography

Explore CMS measure OP-39, Breast Cancer Screening Recall Rates.

What OP-39 measures

OP-39 — Breast Cancer Screening Recall Rates reports the percentage of screening mammograms followed by diagnostic imaging within 45 days. Follow-up may include additional mammography, ultrasound, or MRI.

A recall can be a necessary part of screening, while unnecessary follow-up can add cost and anxiety. The rate alone cannot distinguish appropriate follow-up from avoidable callbacks, and it should not be treated as a stand-alone measure of a hospital’s overall quality.

Understanding Recall Rates

A screening mammogram may identify an area that requires further examination. In these cases, a patient may be called back for additional mammography, ultrasound, or other diagnostic imaging.

Recall rates matter because unnecessary callbacks can create anxiety, extra appointments, and additional costs. However, follow-up imaging can also be essential for investigating suspicious findings.

A lower recall rate is not automatically better. Interpreting these percentages requires clinical context, including the patients being screened and the reasons for additional imaging.
